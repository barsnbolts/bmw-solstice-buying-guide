Before calling anything a bargain

1. Identify the actual assembly
  • Donor VIN, production month, original market, engine code/stamp and recycler stock number.
  • Original engine, replacement or rebuild? Exact generation and emissions variant; longitudinal/transverse; RWD/xDrive; conventional, 48 V or plug-in hybrid.
  • Gearbox tag, BMW part number and ZF/Getrag number; bellhousing/case, output flange or transfer-case interface. A shared “8HP” label is insufficient.
2. Define “dressed” in writing
  • Turbo(s), manifold, intake/charge cooler, throttle, fuel rail, injectors and high-pressure pump(s).
  • Oil pan and pickup, oil/coolant pumps and housings, alternator/starter, brackets/pulleys and A/C compressor if offered.
  • For a gearbox: torque converter or DCT clutch assembly, pan, cooler connections, output parts, and transfer case if advertised. Record missing items individually.
  • External radiators/heat exchangers are not automatically included. Package photos must match the parts being sold.
3. Establish condition and mileage
  • Mileage units and evidence: odometer/VIN record, seller statement, or unknown. An odometer documents the car, not automatically a replacement engine.
  • Cold-start/run video before dismantling; compression/leak-down results with conditions and cylinder values; oil/filter inspection and known oil-pressure history.
  • Crash impact location, broken castings/mounts, sump damage, water/fire exposure, storage and open-port protection, turbo damage, prior tune or track use.
  • Gearbox engagement/shift evidence, fluid condition, fault history and known repairs. “Run & Drive” auction language is not a mechanical warranty.
4. Agree the transaction and delivery
  • Written price/currency, seller identity, stock confirmation, warranty/return terms and any race-use exclusions, core charge and refund conditions.
  • Pickup vs freight, crate, commercial/residential destination, liftgate, insurance, border broker and taxes. Obtain an Ontario delivery quote.
  • Whole donor: title/ownership, buyer eligibility, keys, towing/loading, storage deadlines and fees. Keep resale proceeds separate until actually supported.

The buying decision

Start with a first-generation B58 from a 540i, 740i, X3 M40i or X4 M40i if value matters most. The recycler evidence includes engines with turbos at US$4,700–5,660, before freight and taxes. These share the useful B58 architecture; that does not establish identical sumps, accessories or exact engine suffixes. A cheap luxury-car donor can be more compelling than an enthusiast-car engine with an identical family name.

Keep the S55 benchmark, but do not pay for the M2 badge automatically. An M3/M4 S55 is a genuine alternative; verify the particular output/hardware variant and dress. The intriguing Canadian lead is an unconfirmed S55/M4 engine lead advertised at C$10,000 and 60,000 km by St-Bruno Auto Parts. Its description conflicts with the S55 interchange by saying “3.9”; engine identity, dressing and condition evidence are incomplete, so this is a verification priority rather than a proven bargain. The US$6,500 M2 Competition engine is damaged, not a healthy-engine comparable.

S58 is the expensive step up; B48/B46 is a real four-cylinder path. S58 SUV donors deserve attention, with a US$11,000 X3 M lead against a US$12,622.50 low-mileage M3 assembly. The 2022 SUV crankshaft update matters. Longitudinal B48/B46 engines retain a four-cylinder architecture closer to the LNF; transverse or hybrid variants need distinct assembly comparisons. No defensible dressed-weight or external-size advantage has been quantified.

Original benchmark: dressed 2020 M2 Competition, about 55,000 km

There is no adequately documented healthy, fully dressed exact-year/55,000-km cohort in the captured evidence. Its empirical mean and median are therefore unavailable. “55,000 km” is about 34,175 miles, not 55,000 miles. All listing mileages remain seller-stated unless an auction labels the odometer actual; none was independently verified against service records.

Two adjacent-mileage 2021 M2 Competition recycler assemblies, at 29,000 and 33,000 miles, ask US$12,495 and US$12,500. Their mean and median are US$12,497.50 (C$17,666); dress is unspecified, so this is an assembly-price reference, not a dressed benchmark. A 2020 M2 Competition at 50,285 miles asks US$10,500 with turbos, but is materially higher mileage. A fully described M4 assembly with turbos/accessories at 87,498 miles asks US$10,495; a single item cannot establish a robust market average.

Search coverage and interpretation

The investigation searched US and Canadian listings across recycler inventory, independent dismantlers, eBay, Kijiji, OfferUp, Craigslist, enthusiast/specialist listings, Copart and auction archives. Live Car-Part searches used All Areas and price ordering, exposing repeated broker listings of the same stock. LKQ inventory appeared through the recycler network; direct LKQ access was limited. Marketplace access did not yield a retained verifiable item. IAA fee evidence was accessible, but no sufficiently documented IAA donor was retained. No adequately documented North American half-cut was found. These are coverage limits, not proof that no such opportunities exist.

Listings are deduplicated by exact item URL and identified stock/donor evidence; uncertain cross-list duplicates remain marked. Multiple parts from one donor are separate items, not duplicate engines. A selected, accessible sample is not a census of all North American inventory. A price visible on a listing page is not confirmation that a seller still possesses the item. No sellers were contacted, no inspections occurred, and no purchases or bids were placed.

Healthy/used asking statistics exclude disclosed damage, cores, rebuilt/core-exchange offerings, ended/archived asks, auction bids and search-only leads. Even retained used-condition listings are seller claims, not independently proven healthy engines. Each statistical cohort names completeness and mileage limitations. Unknown-mileage records stay visible and are never normalized to 55,000 km.

To continue efficiently, search both codes and ordinary terms: S55 motor, M4 part out, 740i engine, 540i motor assembly, B58B30M1, B58B30M2, X5 45e engine, X3 M engine, 530i B46, 330e engine, plus the exact gearbox tag. Search recyclers by donor/year and interchange, then compare origin-yard stock IDs. For each shortlisted item, the unanswered purchasing facts are the donor VIN/production date, documented mileage, damage/run-test evidence, exact attached hardware, transmission tag and an Ontario freight quote. These are evidence needed to evaluate the item, not mandatory ECU/TCU/harness purchases.

Physical differences that matter when comparing bare donors

N55 baseline. BMW’s N55 training material describes an aluminum inline-six using one twin-scroll turbo, an air-gap-insulated 6-into-2 exhaust manifold, centrally located high-precision direct injectors, Valvetronic and Double-VANOS. The common BMW press specifications give 10.2:1 compression; exact suffix/model specifications should govern. It has a map-controlled/variable-flow oil pump, piston-crown oil spray, and a sump/oil-return arrangement intended to keep returning oil away from the crankshaft under lateral acceleration. BMW’s MY2015 U.S. update notice says X5 sDrive35i/xDrive35i production from October 2014 changes from 2,979 to 3,004 cc. The initial MY2015 technical sheet still lists 2,979 cc, but BMW’s MY2016 technical sheet lists the same N55B30M0 in those X5s at 3,004 cc with 84.3 × 89.7 mm bore/stroke; best reading is a verified X5-specific production revision, not a family-wide N55 property. Verify the individual donor’s production date/VIN/parts before assigning this distinction. The special M2 N55B30T0 is another distinct variant: BMW specifies M3/M4-derived pistons and main-bearing shells. Treat it as a comparison point, not proof that all N55s share those internals.

First B58 versus B58TU. The original B58 is a 2,998 cc, 82.0 × 94.6 mm inline-six with 11.0:1 compression in BMW’s MY2016 340i technical material. BMW training describes a heat-treated aluminum, closed-deck crankcase, LDS wire-sprayed cylinder surfaces, deep skirt/reinforcement and map-controlled oil pump. It uses direct injection, Valvetronic, Double-VANOS, a single twin-scroll turbo and indirect charge-air cooling integrated with the intake system. The training material also documents engine-mounted electronics and model-specific external layouts; it does not establish a single transferable accessory-drive package. BMW’s B58TU training manual documents split cooling (separately managed head/crankcase needs), revisions to the fuel system and turbo/exhaust hardware. For B58B30M1/O1 applications it distinguishes cylinder-head-integrated exhaust-manifold versions from non-integrated versions; the exhaust-turbo housing/manifold arrangement is therefore engine-version-specific. B58TU is still direct-injection-only in the BMW material reviewed; “TU” alone does not mean port injection.

B58TU2 / latest B58. BMW calls the 2023 740i engine B58TU2. Its press material identifies Miller-cycle valve timing, revised ports/chambers, electrically controlled VANOS, switchable exhaust-side rocker arms, an exhaust manifold integrated into the head, a 48 V transmission-mounted starter-generator and a dual-injection system. The 2025 X3 M50 uses B58B30M2 and BMW specifically reports a stronger crankshaft drive, revised main bearings/caps, piston oil-cooling-channel supply and dual injection. The same basic engine designation appears on some other 2025 BMWs, but BMW’s public model material does not consistently state the fuel hardware for every one; do not propagate a dual-injection claim by model year alone. The B58 used by the Supra/Grenadier is earlier specification and remains DI-only in their manufacturer technical descriptions.

B46/B48 and TU/TU2. Original North-American B46 is a 2.0 L longitudinal or transverse application depending on chassis; BMW’s X1 and X2 technical sheets identify the SULEV B46, twin-scroll turbo, high-pressure direct injection, Valvetronic and Double-VANOS, with 10.2:1 compression in the cited X1/330i specs. BMW’s B46TU/B48TU training guide says B46TU introduced a revised head, lighter crank, one-piece timing-chain drive, split cooling, electrical heat-management module, 350-bar injection and DME 8. The guide reports up to 1,043 g crankshaft weight reduction on B46TU; the high-output transverse B48A20T1 instead gets a reinforced crank with larger main-bearing diameter, altered-compression piston/rods, larger turbo, higher-flow 350-bar injectors and revised intake/exhaust. These are real donor differences inside the “B48” family.

BMW’s MY2025 press documents identify B48B20O2 in the 330i/430i and X3 30; BMW explicitly states the X3 30’s new dual system adds low-pressure injection to the existing high-pressure direct injection. The 2025 2-Series Gran Coupe release identifies B48A20O2 and dual direct/port injection; the M235 xDrive release also describes dual injection and reinforced M-specific components, but the public material checked does not provide its full engine designation. A shared type code makes dual injection likely for other same-code O2 applications, but where BMW’s own application release does not state it, that extension remains an inference. Pre-O2 BMW sources specify High Precision/direct injection only.

Cooling, oiling, accessories and orientation. BMW training confirms N55 model-specific oil-pan/snorkel adaptation and the B58/B48 TU split-cooling/heat-management changes. BMW’s X3 M50 material confirms revised piston-cooling oil supply on that M2 variant. None of the sources establishes a single swappable sump, accessory drive, head, turbo, wiring or calibration across a family. In particular, transverse B46/B48, hybrid B48/B58, SULEV exhaust treatment and SUV xDrive sump/accessory packaging need exact-model parts evidence before being treated as interchangeable. This research did not audit those part numbers.

Physical distinctions that matter to a donor search

DCT versus 8HP. GS7D36-series M DCT is a dual-clutch gearbox with two wet clutches and nested input shafts; it is not a torque-converter automatic. BMW F80/F82 training material says the housing and shaft bearing are based on GS7D36BG and that the engine connection (bellhousing) was adapted for S55. Separately, a BMW parts-catalog mirror labels the F80 M3 DCT diagram GS7D36SG, and the N55 F87 M2 DCT diagram is also GS7D36SG. Preserve this source/code conflict: it does not establish that S55 and N55 units share every physical interface, nor that seller use of GS7D36SG makes a F80/F82 donor impossible. A code string by itself is insufficient to accept or reject fit. Obtain the photographed transmission tag, BMW part number, and donor VIN/build record.

RWD versus AWD. BMW parts identifiers commonly use Z for longitudinal RWD variants and X/X-drive for AWD variants, while individual BMW internal codes do not follow one universal text pattern. A RWD transmission normally terminates in its rear propeller-shaft output arrangement. The AWD unit must mate to the vehicle's transfer-case/front-drive takeoff, with corresponding case/output and packaging changes. An AWD gearbox therefore is not simply a RWD box with an extra shaft, and a listing that says “AWD” while omitting the X code is not a verified X unit. The associated transfer case is a separate assembly and is often not included in a used-transmission listing. Never infer interchange from 8HP50/8HP51 family alone.

Converter versus hybrid motor. Conventional ZF 8HP installations use a torque converter/lock-up clutch and model-specific converter, flexplate/drive plate, bellhousing, input, and pump parts. BMW parts diagrams identify converter assemblies for conventional ZF automatics. The 330e GA8P75HZ instead packages an electric machine, separation clutch, and associated torsional damping where the standard automatic has a converter arrangement. BMW G20 PHEV training documentation describes this different layout. A marketplace catalog field that says “torque converter” is not proof that the PHEV unit includes one. ZF 8HP HIS denotes hydraulic impulse storage for stop-start operation; it does not mean hybrid propulsion. ZF's later 8P75H/8P75PH is hybrid nomenclature, distinct from ordinary 8HP75.

Bellhousing and other interfaces. BMW application-specific transmission variants can differ at the engine-side housing/interface, converter/flexplate or hybrid separation assembly, input shaft, mechatronics/software, sump/cooling connections, mounts, output flange, and AWD transfer-case joint. This research found no authoritative universal dimensions or bolt-pattern table sufficient to design a swap. Obtain the exact transmission tag and BMW part number and inspect the mating components; this report does not state dimensions or torque ratings by nominal family number.

Family labels and near-matches

ZF's 2026 reman catalogue lists 8HP45, 8HP50, 8HP51, 8HP70, 8HP75, 8HP76 and additional families, including Z/X, HIS, or hybrid entries where offered. ZF's current TE-ML 11 service list separately names 8HP80, 8HP80MH/XMH, 8P80PH, and 8P80XPH. ZF's older North American 2018 reman catalogue contains older 8HP45/45X, 8HP55A, 8HP70/70X/70HIS, 8HP90 and 8P70H sections. These catalogues establish serviced families; they are not interchange charts or a vehicle/engine approval. With fabrication allowed, V8/diesel transmission-only units can be investigated as adaptation donors, but remain mechanically and electronically unqualified for target engines until specific work verifies them.

The 2015 BMW X5 U.S. technical table is a useful guard against false assignment: N55B30M0 X5 sDrive35i and xDrive35i use 8HP45, while the adjacent N63B44O1 xDrive50i and N57D30O1 xDrive35d use 8HP75. BMW U.S. 2017 5 Series data pairs B46 530i and B58 540i with 8HP50. A 2016 7 Series U.S. technical sheet similarly pairs 740i with 8HP50 and 750i xDrive with 8HP75. A priced 2013–15 750i xDrive listing advertises an 8HP70X. Thus 8HP70/75 donor transmissions are real market candidates for a fabrication-led project, but are not factory pairings to the target engine families on the evidence reviewed. The evidence does not support calling N55 X5 a 70/75 or S58 F97/F98 a 75. Exact VIN/build records could expand these bounded examples.

Fourth-generation PHEV extension

BMW parts-catalog views identify a newer 8P80 PHEV branch distinct from the older GA8P75HZ/8P75H. The G05 LCI X5 50e xDrive (including USA catalog vehicle code 43EU) uses GA8HP80XPH; G60 530e RWD catalog records use GA8P80PH, while 530eX uses GA8HP80XPH; the G60 550eX (including a USA market type code in the catalog) is listed with GA8HP80XPH; and G70 750eX parts-catalog material lists GA8HP80XPH. BMW Canada confirms the 750e xDrive model and an electric motor integrated in the eight-speed transmission. The precise sales availability of each 530e/550e/750e variant differs by national market and production date; catalog records alone do not prove every trim was retailed in both the U.S. and Canada.

ZF's April 2026 TE-ML 11 list names 8P80PH and 8P80XPH separately from conventional/mild-hybrid 8HP80 / 8HP80MH groups. ZF describes Gen4 8HP as modular across combustion, mild-hybrid, and plug-in-hybrid applications. In the PHEV branch, the electric machine is packaged in the transmission, with model-specific power electronics, stator/rotor, oil-pan, mechatronic, and output parts. Do not take the substring “HP80” as evidence that a GA8HP80XPH is a conventional torque-converter 8HP80 or mechanically interchangeable with the 8HP80MH/XMH.
